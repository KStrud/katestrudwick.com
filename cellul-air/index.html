Cellul—air

Cellul—air

Air-purifying bacteria brought out into public space.

Back
Imperial College × RCA, 2019

Bringing bacteria into public spaces to help in the fight against air pollution.

Today’s air filters, like HEPA, work well but aren’t sustainable: used filters are incinerated, adding to the pollution they were meant to remove. They’re also hidden inside buildings, which makes it easy to ignore the air around us.

I split my time between scientific research, material development and aesthetic direction. Because the project is speculative, I built physical prototypes, digital artwork and animation to show a future where Cellul—air could exist.

The result is a visible, modular filtration system filled with air-purifying bacteria, tuned to the pollutants in a specific space. The bacteria and the material they grow on are biodegradable and the modules are reusable, so the system produces no waste.
